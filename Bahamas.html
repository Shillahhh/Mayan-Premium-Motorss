All you need to know about Bahamas

1.The History of Bahamas

History Before European arrival: The islands were inhabited primarily by the Lucayan people, a branch of the Taíno-speaking peoples of the Caribbean. They lived by fishing, farming, and trade. 1492 – Christopher Columbus: Columbus arrived in the Bahamas during his first voyage to the Americas. He is generally believed to have first landed on an island he called San Salvador. 1500s–1600s: European diseases, enslavement, and forced relocation devastated the Lucayan population. By the early 1500s, the indigenous population had largely disappeared. 1600s–1700s: The islands became associated with European colonial powers and later came under British control. The Bahamas also became known for pirates because of its strategic position along important Atlantic shipping routes. 1700s–1800s: The British strengthened their control. After the American Revolution, many Loyalists moved to the Bahamas, bringing enslaved Africans with them and influencing the islands' population and economy. 1834 – Abolition of slavery: Slavery was abolished throughout the British Empire, including the Bahamas, although the transition to full social and economic equality was gradual. 1900s: The Bahamas developed increasingly as a tourist destination. The islands also became an important financial center. 1973 – Independence: The Bahamas became an independent country on July 10, 1973, while remaining a member of the Commonwealth. It retained the British monarch as its constitutional head of state,represented locally by governer-general.

2.Geography The Bahamas is an archipelago, meaning it is made up of many islands and smaller cays. Important islands include: New Providence – home to Nassau and the country's main population center. Grand Bahama – home to Freeport, an important commercial and tourism center. Abaco Islands Eleuthera Exuma Andros – the largest island by land area. The country is famous for its white-sand beaches, coral reefs, clear turquoise waters, and extensive marine ecosystems.

3. Government and Politics. The Bahamas is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy. Head of state: King Charles III, represented by a governor-general. Head of government: The prime minister. Parliament: Bicameral, consisting of the House of Assembly and the Senate. The country operates under a constitutional system based largely on the Westminster parliamentary tradition inherited from Britain.

4. Population and Society. The Bahamas has a population of roughly 400,000 people, concentrated heavily around New Providence. The population is predominantly of African descent, with smaller communities of European, Asian, and mixed ancestry. Official language: English Religion: Christianity is predominant, with various Protestant denominations and Roman Catholic communities. Capital: Nassau Currency: Bahamian dollar (BSD), which is maintained at parity with the U.S. dollar.
